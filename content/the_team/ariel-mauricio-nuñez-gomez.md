 Ariel brings fifteen years of experience in open source geospatial technology, combining hands-on engineering with a proven record of selling it. His journey began as an electronic engineer in Barranquilla, Colombia, where early projects — a fleet management system for the Cerrejón coal mine and a mapping platform for the Colombian Ministry of Agriculture — drew him into GIS.

 Trained at Universidad del Norte, with postgraduate studies in computer vision, Ariel is the rare salesperson who can also run the demo, scope the architecture and deliver the project. This builder's perspective means clients get honest answers about where open source tools fit — and where they don't.

 In 2010 he joined the World Bank's Global Facility for Disaster Reduction and Recovery (GFDRR), where he became one of the core developers of GeoNode and spent six years training government agencies in more than fifteen countries on open source GIS and open data. In 2015 he founded Piensa Labs, his own consultancy, selling and delivering open source geospatial projects for the World Bank, the Inter-American Development Bank, GIZ, and universities including Harvard, Stanford and KAUST — much of it in climate adaptation and disaster risk reduction.

 As a long-time contributor to GeoNode, GeoServer and QGIS plugins like InaSAFE, Ariel is deeply committed to the open source geospatial ecosystem. He believes the strongest case for open source is made by the people who build it: no licence fees, no lock-in, and the freedom for institutions to own their own infrastructure.

 As Sales Manager, Ariel leads Kartoza's sales strategy. His blend of engineering depth, community standing and commercial experience means that what Kartoza sells, it can build.
